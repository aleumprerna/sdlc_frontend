# Aelum-inspired UI and UX

## Work from the project

1. Inspect the existing design system, routes, framework, components, copy, assets, and evidence. Identify the primary audience, task, and next step. Make reversible assumptions and surface consequential decisions at delivery.
2. Choose a page recipe. Tie each section to a question the audience has or an action they need. Keep existing routes and working feature behavior during a redesign.
3. Define layout rhythm, semantic tokens, imagery direction, and CTA hierarchy before implementation. Use original compositions, assets, and copy. Carry over the reference's editorial pacing, outcome-led writing, and proof placement.
4. Write finished copy while composing screens. Connect links, forms, tabs, and filters through their normal entry points. For a design-only brief, deliver responsive section structure, sample copy, component states, and behavior.
5. Render and exercise the relevant screens and report unverified states. Use the framework and components already present when they fit.

## Pick a page recipe

| Surface | Section order and decisions |
| --- | --- |
| Homepage or landing page | Outcome-led hero and one primary action → verified early proof → offer or capabilities → sector, audience, or workflow paths → featured story → optional relevant resources → next step. |
| Services hub | Define the offering → group engagement modes or service categories → compare scope and link to details → show relevant proof → route to consultation. |
| Service detail | Say what the buyer receives → describe the problem and expected change → break down deliverables or delivery phases → show application by audience → add matched proof and decision FAQs → offer an assessment or conversation. |
| Solution | Name the operational problem → explain the connected outcome and updated workflow → group capabilities → show relevant evidence → link to related solutions when those links advance the decision. |
| Industry | Lead with a sector-specific operational reality → map pain points to workflows and outcomes → show applicable capabilities and segments → add same-sector proof and FAQs → propose a next step. |
| Case study list | Feature a result-led story, then scannable cards. Add search and meaningful facets only when the library warrants them. Show active filters, reset, and empty state. |
| Case study detail | Lead with the result → identify the organization or approved anonymized context → describe challenge and objective → explain intervention → give observed outcomes, measurement context, and relevant limits → show related stories. |
| Insights or article | Group by format or topic. Give cards a title, short hook, and useful metadata. In an article, open with a recognizable situation, answer the question early, use practical section headings, and link related reading. |
| Company and partners | Explain who does the work → show meaningful history, team, or principles → clarify what each partner enables and verify the credentials → route to a next step. |
| Careers | Describe work and team practices → help applicants discover jobs → state file requirements before upload → provide validation, progress, and recovery. |
| Contact | Pair a reassuring next step and alternate channels with a labeled form. Separate inquiry types only when routing differs. Explain what happens after submission. |
| App, portal, or dashboard | Lead with the current task, primary action, and status → show relevant data or work queue → provide clear filters and recovery states. Apply typography, spacing, and copy principles. Keep the interface centered on the task. |

## Design and interaction

- Honor existing brand tokens. For a greenfield concept, start with deep blue-green or another brand-appropriate dark surface, off-white content areas, charcoal text, one saturated interactive accent, and optional bright emphasis on dark surfaces. Define tokens by purpose and verify foreground/background contrast in each state.
- Use a confident sans-serif hierarchy. For a greenfield starting point, use 1120–1280 px content width, 20–40 px side gutters, 64–104 px section spacing on desktop and 40–64 px on mobile. Try 52–64 px hero type on desktop and 36–46 px on mobile, 34–48 px section headings on desktop and 26–36 px on mobile, and 16–18 px body with roughly 1.5 line height. Size to content and preserve readable line lengths.
- Alternate open editorial sections with darker emphasis bands or structured card grids. Use realistic original or authorized photography tied to the content. Leave a calm text zone and set a mobile crop or fallback. Reserve full-bleed visuals for moments with a clear purpose.
- Use one primary action per decision point. Choose filled primary controls, quieter secondary actions, and descriptive drill-down links. Use 2–4 columns for comparable cards when space supports them and stack in content order. Keep card anatomy consistent within each group.
- Use short global navigation with descriptive categories. Use tabs for comparable views and accordions for independent questions. Keep important actions outside carousels and accessible without hover. Give menus, tabs, and accordions semantic controls, keyboard behavior, visible focus, and programmatically exposed selected or expanded state.
- Label fields above their controls. Identify required fields and meaningful hints, show inline errors, preserve entered values on recoverable failures, and announce status. Handle loading, empty, error, success, focus, hover, and disabled or pending states where applicable. Reflow forms to one column on phones and respect reduced motion.

## Copy templates

| Element | Format | Original example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | 4–10 words naming a practical outcome, then one sentence explaining audience, mechanism, or payoff | **Resolve requests before they pile up.** Give support leads one queue for incoming requests, ownership, and handoffs. |
| Service card | Short capability name → one sentence explaining what changes → specific link | **Workflow assessment.** Find where requests stall and which handoffs to fix first. **Explore the assessment** |
| Industry lead | Recognizable situation → change in workflow → practical benefit | When maintenance calls reach three teams, the line can stay down while people find an owner. Route each fault to the right crew as soon as it is logged. |
| Proof | Verified result or qualification → scope → evidence source when available | **One intake queue across both sites.** From the approved rollout review. |
| Case title | Outcome plus organization or operational context | **How a field team brought dispatch into one queue.** |
| Resource | Reader question or task → specific promise | **How to spot delays in incident handoffs.** A practical review for leads who manage several queues. |
| Contact | Make an early brief feel easy → explain the next step | **Tell us where work gets stuck.** Share a workflow or a goal and we’ll use it to start the conversation. |
| UI feedback | Say what happened → give the next useful action | **No stories match these filters.** Clear a filter to see more. / **Enter a work email address.** / **Request sent.** We’ll reply to the email you provided. |

Use active verbs, concrete nouns, and sentence case unless the project specifies another convention. Write in a conversational tone for inquiries, precise terms for capabilities, and direct task language for product UI. Use project-approved claims, logos, quotes, metrics, and client names. When proof is absent, describe a verified process or omit the proof block.

## Verify before delivery

- Inspect a representative wide and narrow render. Check long titles, image crops, logical section order, nav, form reflow, keyboard focus, contrast, tap targets, horizontal overflow, and zoom where practical.
- Exercise links, menus, tabs, filters, form validation and feedback, reduced motion, and keyboard paths as applicable. Check loading, empty and recoverable error states for data-dependent screens.
- Confirm that readers can tell who the page is for, what changes for them, what evidence backs it, and what to do next. For apps, confirm users can see their task and its current state.
